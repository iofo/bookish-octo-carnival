Investment assumption

Total nominal return each year = inflation + capital gain over inflation + qualified dividend yield.

Dividend yield adds to total return on top of inflation and capital gain — raising it increases your overall return, not just its composition. Only affects the Taxable (Brokerage) option: dividends are taxed annually at capital gains rates, so only the after-tax portion gets reinvested. Traditional and Roth are unaffected, since neither is taxed on internal account activity.

Year-by-year contribution detail
Future value = that year's savings amount, compounded at the expected return until retirement. % of pot = that year's future value as a share of all contribution future values combined.
Federal tax is an estimate only: single-filer, 2026 IRS brackets and standard deduction ($16,100), with the deduction and bracket thresholds assumed to rise with inflation while the rates themselves stay fixed. It ignores state/local tax, filing status, credits, and other deductions — not tax advice. Pre-tax (Traditional) reduces that year's taxable income by the savings amount before computing tax, matching a traditional 401(k)/IRA. After-tax (Roth) and Taxable (Brokerage) both tax the full salary with no deduction — the difference between them is entirely at withdrawal, not on the way in. FICA (est.) is 2026 Social Security (6.2% up to the $184,500 wage base) plus Medicare (1.45–2.35%), applied to gross wages regardless of the contribution-type toggle. Both Federal tax and FICA are subtracted to get net take-home; FICA never affects any other figure on the page. Equalize net pay (Roth/Taxable only): reduces the contribution amount so that year's net take-home matches what Pre-tax would give at the same savings-rate schedule. The Savings % and Savings amount columns reflect this adjusted, smaller contribution — which is why Roth/Taxable balances often end up lower than Traditional ones for the "same" paycheck impact. Taxable (Brokerage) withdrawal tax: assumes a buy-and-hold account where gains are only realized at withdrawal, taxed as 2026 long-term capital gains (0%/15%/20% brackets, inflation-adjusted) plus the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax above $200,000 MAGI (a fixed threshold — by law it has never been inflation-indexed). Each year's withdrawal is assumed to carry gain in the same proportion as the account's overall gain-to-balance ratio; your original contributions come back tax-free as return of basis.
